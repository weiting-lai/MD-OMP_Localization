05 / Paper & Companion Demo

Localisation Is the Research Result

The paper asks where sound sources are in a reverberant environment. This page makes those position estimates visible and provides an audible example of how they can be used.

Proposed in the paper

MD-OMP estimates source positions

Multi-Dictionary Orthogonal Matching Pursuit jointly exploits spatial, temporal and spectral information to recover 3D source locations. The paper evaluates localisation accuracy through simulations and real-room measurements.

The measured coordinates, estimated coordinates and 3D errors shown here are the localisation result from the recorded experiment.

Added for this companion

An audible use of those estimates

The saved MD-OMP coordinates guide an offline source-extraction pipeline. Selecting a marker plays the corresponding extracted recording, linking a location in the room to its speech content.

This extraction pipeline and stereo player were added for the demonstration. They illustrate the practical use of the locations; the paper’s contribution and evaluation concern localisation.

06 / Recorded Experiment

Four Sources in a Reverberant Room

Four loudspeakers play speech in a furnished office at ANU. Fourteen microphones along the room perimeter capture the sound field together.

A real acoustic scene

4.35 × 3 × 3 m office, with furniture and reflective surfaces in place. The paper reports approximately 0.73 s reverberation time using T₂₀ extrapolation.

The localisation input

The saved MD-OMP result uses an approximately 3–5 s interval, 61 frequency bins from 125 to 2,000 Hz, and a 30 × 30 × 5 candidate grid.

One result, two ways to explore it

Compare estimated and measured positions in the room view and table. Then audition the supporting audio, processed from the full 12.608-second recording using the estimated coordinates.

Listening pipeline, output checks & scope

The estimated positions guide filter initialisation, a spatial prior during ILRMA updates, and the final association between extracted tracks and locations. All four estimated coordinates are used; speech is extracted from the actual 14-channel recording.

Processing uses WPE (2,048-point FFT, 256-sample hop, 20 taps), spatially regularised ILRMA (4,096-point FFT, two NMF bases, 150 iterations), mask-based MVDR. This is the tested MD-OMP-position configuration with the highest mean STOI among the final estimated-position candidates.

Mean STOI for this listening example is 0.704. This auxiliary check of the extracted speech is separate from the paper’s localisation metrics. Residual interference and distortion remain, particularly for S1 and S4.

Source tracks are saved as centred stereo WAVs. The all-source mix is their sum with a fixed gain of one quarter, preserving headroom. Clean playback clips are used only to evaluate the output; they are not used to generate these audio files. Measured room-transfer filters are not used.
